The problem

Search and recommendations ran, but nobody could steer, test or trust them.

Ranking nobody could steer

Merchandisers couldn't see why a product ranked where it did, so every change became an engineering ticket.

No way to test

There was no A/B testing in search, merchandising or recommendations, so every change was a judgment call.

A catalog out of sync

Product data reached the old engine through a separate feed and drifted from the source.

What I did

Defined the platform before picking a vendor

I wrote 87 requirements across search, merchandising, recommendations and the platform itself, and split them into MVP and later phases. Every demo and every vendor score used them, which kept the decision about our problems rather than the best demo.

Designed the browse experience

Curated filters per category, six to nine per page. Merchandising rules sit on top of the ranking model, so campaigns survive model updates, and sold-out products stay visible but rank low.

Rebuilt recommendations around business goals

I audited every existing placement and proposed a new strategy. Each placement gets one goal, a primary strategy, a fallback and guardrails. Search keeps sold-out items ranked low, while recommendations leave them out.

What's working

  • One set of requirements kept the vendor choice about our problems, not the best demo.
  • Fixing catalog data before the switch, so the new engine doesn't inherit old errors.

Risks I'm managing

  • Revenue wasn't tracked in the proof phase. Tracking is rebuilt before launch so results can be compared at all.
  • The success target sits close to what can be reliably detected, so it's read against a locked baseline over 16 weeks.

What I'd do differently

I'd agree the measurement design before signing, not during the build. Two tracking gaps only surfaced in implementation, and both would have shaped how we defined success.
