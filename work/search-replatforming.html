Carhartt WIP, 2026

Replatforming search and discovery

Carhartt WIP is moving search, browse and recommendations to a new engine, starting with Germany. I led the choice of engine, then the harder part: getting the catalog, the merchandising rules and the measurement ready for it.

The problem

Search and recommendations worked, but nobody could steer them.

Ranking nobody could explain

Merchandisers couldn't see why a product ranked where it did, so every fix became an engineering ticket.

An engine that saw a different catalog

Product data arrived through a separate feed. Names drifted from the source and new arrivals kept losing their dates.

Recommendations on autopilot

Placements ran on old rules that no one owned, tested or measured.

What I did

One brief, six engines

I wrote a single brief covering what the business needed: ranking we could explain, merchandising across markets, recommendations, and tests teams could run without engineering. Six engines were scored against it. I then led the commercial negotiation, including a success target we could measure.

Fix the catalog before the launch

The old engine received product data through a separate feed, so names and values drifted from the source. A date bug kept resetting which products counted as new. We fixed both before switching, so the new engine starts clean.

Browse: rules that survive the model

Filters come from a curated set of attributes, six to nine per page, with category rules on top. Merchandising boosts sit above the learned ranking, so campaigns survive model updates, and sold-out products stay visible but rank low.

What worked

  • Scoring every engine against one brief kept the decision about our problems, not the best demo.
  • Fixing catalog data before the switch, so the new engine didn't inherit old errors.

What didn't

  • Revenue was never tracked in the proof phase, so early results couldn't be compared until tracking was rebuilt.
  • The measurement design came after the contract, which limited how precisely we could define success.

What I'd do differently

I'd agree the measurement design before signing, not during the build. Two tracking gaps only surfaced in implementation, and both would have shaped how we defined success.
